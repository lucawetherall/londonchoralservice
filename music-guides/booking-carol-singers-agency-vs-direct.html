Booking carol singers: agency or direct?

By Luca Wetherall, Artistic Director & Tutor in Music, University of Oxford

Last updated: July 2026

There are two ways to get carol singers into your December. You can go through an entertainment agency or a booking marketplace, which lists many acts and lets you compare them in one place. Or you can go direct to a choir that runs its own diary and deal with the musicians who will sing. Both routes get singers through the door. They differ in what you can find out before you commit, and in how much of the price you can see. This guide covers how each one works, and what to ask whichever you choose.

How the carol-singing market is organised

Search for carol singers in October and most of what comes back belongs to entertainment agencies and booking marketplaces. These are platforms rather than performers. They carry profiles for hundreds of acts — jazz trios, string quartets, function bands, carol groups — and take your enquiry on behalf of whichever ones are free on your date and inside your budget. The act you end up booking is an independent supplier listed on the platform, and the platform handles the introduction, the contract, and usually the payment.

Alongside the platforms sit choirs and ensembles that take their own bookings. The London Choral Service is one of these. So are chamber choirs, professional consorts, and groups built around a fixed roster of singers under a named director. They are harder to find in one search, because nothing aggregates them onto a single page and they tend to rank for their own name rather than for “carol singers for hire”.

Neither structure is better in the abstract. They suit different jobs, and the sensible question is which one suits yours.

What the agency route gives you

One enquiry, many acts. You describe the date, the venue, and the budget once, and hear back from several groups. For a first-time booker with no idea what a carol quartet costs, that is a fast way to calibrate.

Like-for-like comparison. Profiles on a platform follow the same template, so you can line up ensemble sizes, set lengths, and headline fees against each other without translating five different quote formats.

A familiar way to pay. Marketplaces carry messaging threads, standard contracts, card payment, and a review history for each act. If your finance team prefers a supplier with a payment platform behind it, that matters.

Bundling. If your party needs a choir, a DJ, and a photographer, doing all three through one platform saves you two conversations and one set of paperwork.

What a listing cannot tell you

A profile page is a shop window for a group, and there are three things it will rarely answer without you asking.

Which singers arrive on the night. The photograph shows four people. The four who turn up on the eleventh of December may not be those four, because December is the busiest fortnight in the choral calendar and singers are juggling other work. Ask who is confirmed, and when you will know.

Who directs the group. Somebody chooses the arrangements, sets the balance in the room, decides where the singers stand, and calls the running order when the speeches overrun by twenty minutes. Ask who that person is and whether they are in the room.

Whether the group sings together regularly. Some carol groups are settled ensembles. Others are assembled per booking from a pool of freelancers. Pickup groups of good singers can be excellent, but only when the singers already know one another’s voices, so it is a fair thing to ask about.

None of this is a mark against agencies. The information sits with the act, not the platform, which is why the platform cannot publish it for you.

What booking direct with a choir means

Going direct means the person answering your email is the person responsible for the music. With The London Choral Service, that is Luca Wetherall, our Artistic Director. He auditioned every singer on the team himself, picks the ensemble for your date, and plans the programme with you. If you want the Sussex Carol before the chairman speaks and something quiet under the mulled wine afterwards, you are discussing that with the musician who will direct it, not with a middleman relaying it.

We book ensembles from singers who have worked together wherever the December calendar allows, rather than assembling a group from whoever is free. If a singer falls ill, we replace them from within the same team instead of cancelling on you.

The other difference is the price. No intermediary layer sits inside our fee, and the figures are published rather than quoted on request. You can read them before you speak to us.

Questions to ask, whichever route you take

These five work on an agency, a marketplace act, and a choir taking its own bookings. Ask them in writing.

  • Will the singers I am looking at be the singers who perform? If the answer is that the line-up is confirmed nearer the date, ask when you will get the names and what the standard is guaranteed to be.
  • Who directs the group on the night? A named director who is present is a good sign. So is a clear answer about who runs the sound check and who talks to your event manager when the timings slip.
  • Can I hear this group, not a showreel? A recording of the ensemble you are booking tells you about blend and tuning. A general brand montage tells you much less.
  • What does the price cover? Get the total after travel, taxes, and any booking or platform fee. Comparing a headline figure against an all-in figure flatters one of them.
  • What happens if a singer is ill in December? Ask how deputies are found and whether the fee changes. Any provider working at this level should have an answer ready.

Price transparency

Typical agency carol-group bookings run to around four figures, which is the right order of magnitude for four to six professional singers for an evening. The trouble with comparing quotes is that the headline number and the invoice number are not always the same: travel, VAT, and platform fees sit inside some quotes and outside others.

Ours are published on the pricing page. A Small Choir of four is £1,150, a quintet £1,400, a sextet £1,600, a full choir of eight £2,000, and a twelve-voice chorus £3,000; a soloist is £250. Those figures include all taxes and fees, and travel within Greater London. They do not go up for December — we charge no Christmas uplift. Christmas Eve and Christmas Day carry a premium, typically around 25 per cent, confirmed in your written quote before you commit to anything.

If you are weighing us against a quote from a platform, the useful exercise is to put both totals on the same all-in basis and then ask both providers the five questions above.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to book carol singers directly or through an agency?

Not always, and anyone who promises a flat saving is guessing. Agencies price differently from one another, and what you pay turns mostly on ensemble size, the date, and how long the singers perform. What booking direct changes is transparency: there is no intermediary layer in the fee, and you can see what the money covers and who it pays. Our prices are published on our pricing page rather than quoted on request.

How do you know if carol singers are professional?

Ask two questions. Where did the singers train, and where do they sing for the rest of the year? The professional end of the market is staffed by conservatoire graduates and cathedral and Oxbridge choral singers who do consort work year-round. Then ask for a recording of the group you are actually booking, rather than a general showreel for the brand. Our guide to what to look for when hiring Christmas carol singers covers the rest of the checks.

Can I book The London Choral Service directly?

Yes. We take every booking ourselves, so there is no agency between you and the singers. Send the date, the venue, and a rough headcount through our contact page and we reply within 48 hours, usually the same day, with a written quote and two or three programme options. December dates start closing out from mid-September, so early is better.

What should you check before booking carol singers?

Four things. Whether the singers advertised are the singers who will perform; who directs the group on the night; what the quoted price covers once travel, taxes, and any booking fee are counted; and what happens if someone falls ill. Get the answers in writing before you pay a deposit.
