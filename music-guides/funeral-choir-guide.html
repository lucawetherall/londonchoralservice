What to expect from a funeral choir

By Luca Wetherall, Artistic Director & Tutor in Music, University of Oxford

Published 6 March 2026 · Updated 24 October 2026

This guide is for families who have not had a choir at a funeral before. It covers what the singers do during the service, how many you need, what they sing, and what happens on the day, from the time they arrive to the last piece as the coffin leaves. Our funeral choir hire page lists the packages and prices.

What does a funeral choir do? A funeral choir leads the congregation in the hymns, sings up to three pieces of its own, such as Schubert’s Ave Maria or Fauré’s Pie Jesu, and sings or plays as people arrive and leave. Our singers are in the building 45 to 60 minutes early to rehearse. A soloist costs £250, a choir of four £1,150 and a choir of eight £2,000.

We provide singers and choirs for funerals in London and across the UK, often at a few days’ notice. An organist is £250 on top, so a choir of four with an organist is £1,400.

What a funeral choir actually does

The most important thing a choir does at a funeral is lead the hymns. This sounds simple, and it is — but it changes how confidently the congregation sings.

Think about what happens when a congregation tries to sing without a musical lead. People are emotional, uncertain, self-conscious. The organist plays an introduction, a few brave souls start singing, the sound is thin and hesitant, and by the second verse half the room has given up. This is not a criticism of anyone — it is simply what happens when grieving people are asked to sing in public without support.

With four trained singers in the room, the congregation hears the tune clearly from the first line and has voices to follow. People who say they cannot sing join in, and the hymn holds together to the last verse. Most families who hire a choir do so for this.

The choir also sings at other points in the service. As people come in, it may sing quietly while the room settles. During the service it sings one to three pieces the congregation listens to: Ave Maria, Pie Jesu, In Paradisum or another piece the family has chosen. As the coffin leaves and people follow, it can sing a last piece.

For a wider look at the music itself — which hymns, which songs, what works where — our guides to popular funeral hymns and popular funeral songs cover the most commonly chosen pieces in detail.

How many singers do you need?

This depends on the venue, the congregation, and what you want the music to feel like. There is no single right answer, but here is a practical guide.

A solo singer is the most intimate option. One trained voice, supported by an organist or pianist, can lead hymns, perform solo pieces, and provide gentle music throughout the service. It is ideal for smaller services — a crematorium with 30 or 40 guests, or a quiet family funeral. A solo voice in a small room creates a closeness that larger ensembles cannot replicate. A soloist starts from £250.

A small choir of four singers gives you warmth and richness. Four voices in harmony create a sound that is fuller and more enveloping than a single voice. This is our most popular option for church funerals. It provides strong hymn-leading for congregations of any size and allows for beautiful choral pieces during the service. A small choir starts from £1,150.

A quintet of five singers adds a second soprano to the quartet. The extra voice on the tune helps the congregation in the hymns, and it opens up the many pieces written with two soprano parts. A quintet starts from £1,400.

A sextet of six singers is one of our most cost-effective options if you want the sound of a big choir for less. They produce a far bigger sound than a quartet and provide a musical boost to any service. A sextet starts from £1,600.

A full choir of eight singers is a substantial musical presence. It fills a large church with ease and produces the kind of sound that stops people in their tracks. For a significant funeral with a large congregation, this is the option that delivers the most impact. Eight voices singing In Paradisum or Lux Aeterna in a resonant church fills the building. A full choir starts from £2,000.

A chorus of twelve singers is the most immersive option we offer. It creates a wall of sound that is both powerful and moving. This is typically chosen for memorial services, thanksgiving services, or funerals where the family wants the music to be a central, defining feature of the occasion. A chorus starts from £3,000.

An accompanist can be added to any of our options. For full pricing details, see our pricing page, or our funeral music costs guide explains what everything costs and what you get for it.

The size of the ensemble shapes everything from how the hymns feel to how the congregation sings. We help families choose the right option for their venue and occasion.

What they sing

The repertoire divides naturally into three categories.

Hymns. These are the congregational pieces — The Lord’s My Shepherd, Abide with Me, Dear Lord and Father of Mankind, and many others. The choir leads these, carrying the melody and harmonies so that the congregation feels supported. Most funerals include two or three hymns. Our guide to popular funeral hymns covers the most commonly chosen. For Catholic funerals, our Catholic funeral hymns guide covers the tradition-specific choices.

Solo and choral pieces. These are the pieces the congregation listens to — performed by one or more of the singers as a dedicated musical moment. Ave Maria (Schubert), Pie Jesu (Fauré), Panis Angelicus (Franck), In Paradisum, Lux Aeterna, and The Irish Blessing are among the most requested. These pieces are often the musical highlight of the service — the moment where the room falls silent.

Ambient music. Gentle, unobtrusive music as people enter and leave. This might be quiet choral singing, an organ voluntary, or piano music. Its job is to create atmosphere and hold the space while the congregation settles or departs. It is the frame around the picture, and it matters more than people realise.

If the family would like to include non-hymn songs — popular music, show tunes, classical favourites — our guide to popular funeral songs and our non-religious funeral music guide cover the options.

The practicalities

Booking. A straightforward phone call or email is all it takes to get started. We will ask you about the date, the venue, the approximate time of the service, and any hymns or pieces you already have in mind. If you do not know what music you want yet, that is absolutely fine — we can talk it through and make suggestions. The whole process is designed to be as easy as possible at a time when you have a great deal else to think about.

How far in advance? We can often arrange musicians at short notice — sometimes within 48 hours. Funerals are not planned months ahead, and we understand that. Obviously, the more notice we have, the more flexibility we have with our singers, but please do not hesitate to call even if the funeral is only a few days away.

Coordination. We liaise directly with the funeral director, the officiant, and (where applicable) the church or crematorium. The family should not have to manage any of this. We agree the running order in advance, confirm the hymns and pieces, establish any logistical details about the venue, and make sure everyone knows what is happening and when.

On the day. The singers are in the building 45 to 60 minutes before the service. They introduce themselves to the funeral director and the officiant, rehearse in the venue with the organist, and agree where they will stand. Once the service starts they sing the hymns and pieces in the agreed order without needing any direction from the family. If the order changes at the last moment, they follow the officiant.

The booking steps are the same for any occasion; our guide on how to book a choir covers quotes, deposits and choosing the music.

Churches and crematoriums

A funeral choir adapts to both settings, but they feel quite different.

In a church, the acoustics are usually generous — stone walls, vaulted ceilings, natural reverberation that makes voices ring and sustain. A choir of four can sound like eight in a resonant church. There is typically a pipe organ or a good electronic instrument, and the congregation may be larger. The music has room to breathe and expand.

In a crematorium, the space is smaller, the acoustics are drier, and the congregation is often more intimate. A solo singer or a small choir of four is ideal here — filling the room warmly without overwhelming it. The closeness of the space can be a strength: a voice just a few metres away has an immediacy that a large church cannot quite match. Our guide to crematorium music covers this setting in detail, with advice on making the most of a shorter service.

Our singers adjust to the room they are in: they sing more quietly in a small crematorium chapel and give more in a large church with a big congregation.

What it actually feels like

When the choir starts the first hymn, the congregation has something to do together. People who have sat still through the readings stand, find the words in the order of service and sing, with the choir’s voices close enough to follow.

Frequently asked questions

What does a funeral choir do?

A funeral choir leads the congregation in the hymns, sings up to three pieces of its own, such as Schubert’s Ave Maria or Fauré’s Pie Jesu, and sings or plays as people arrive and leave.

How many singers are in a funeral choir?

Anything from one singer to twelve. A choir of four is the size families choose most for a church funeral, and a soloist suits a smaller crematorium service.

What time do funeral singers arrive?

Our singers are in the building 45 to 60 minutes before the service. They rehearse in the venue with the organist, agree where they will stand, and introduce themselves to the funeral director and the officiant.

Do we need an organist as well as a choir?

Usually, to accompany the hymns. Many churches have their own organist, booked through the church. If not, we provide one for £250, so a choir of four with an organist is £1,400.

When should I book a funeral choir?

As soon as the date and venue are set. We can often arrange singers at short notice, sometimes within 48 hours, so get in touch even if the funeral is only a few days away.
