Hear our musicians

A selection of the pieces families request most often, performed by our singers and instrumentalists.

Abide With Me — Full Choir

One of the most beloved funeral hymns in the English tradition. The choral harmonies bring a depth of consolation and warmth that is moving in a service setting.

Abide With Me (words Henry Francis Lyte), sung by our full choir.

He Shall Feed His Flock — Soloist, from Handel’s Messiah

One of the most tender and consoling arias in all of Handel’s Messiah. Gentle, unhurried, and peaceful.

He Shall Feed His Flock, from Handel’s Messiah, sung by a soloist.

Ubi Caritas — Quintet, Ola Gjeilo

A modern setting of the Latin antiphon “where charity and love are, God is there.” Sung unaccompanied, so it needs no organ. Families most often place it at the entry of the coffin or as a reflection after the eulogy.

Ubi Caritas by Ola Gjeilo, sung by a quintet.

Anima Christi — Marco Frisina

The Latin prayer that asks to be called in the hour of death, set by Monsignor Marco Frisina and sung here by a quintet. Catholic families choose it for the Communion of a Requiem Mass or the Final Commendation, sung by the choir while the congregation listens. More in our guide to Anima Christi at a Catholic funeral.

Anima Christi by Marco Frisina, sung by a quintet.

There’s a Wideness in God’s Mercy — Chorus of twelve, tune Coverdale

Frederick William Faber’s hymn on a mercy “like the wideness of the sea”. Its words comfort without dwelling on grief, and with twelve singers leading, a large congregation has a firm line to follow.

There’s a Wideness in God’s Mercy (words Frederick William Faber, tune Coverdale), sung by a chorus of twelve.

The Lord’s My Shepherd — Small Choir

With Abide With Me, one of the two hymns most chosen at UK funerals, sung here to the well-known Crimond melody. Gentle, familiar, and comforting.

What we provide

Every funeral booking includes a personal consultation with Luca Wetherall, our Artistic Director. He will talk through your music choices, suggest repertoire that suits the tone you want, and handle coordination with your funeral director and officiant. You receive a confirmed running order at least 48 hours before the service.

On the day, our singers rehearse in the venue, check acoustics, and confirm positions with the officiant. During the service, they lead all of the congregational hymns and perform up to three choral or solo pieces. After the service, they can provide reflective music as mourners leave.

We perform in churches, crematoriums, chapels, synagogues, and outdoor spaces across the UK. Most families complete the whole process in two or three short conversations. Most families contact us one or two weeks before the service, but short notice is fine, and we have arranged singers at a few hours’ notice.

We sing at churches, crematoriums, and chapels across the UK — most often in London, Oxford, and Manchester, but we travel further when families need us.

Many of our bookings are for non-religious or humanist services — celebrations of life held at crematoriums, woodland venues, or community halls. We adapt the music accordingly: secular songs and instrumental pieces work as well as hymns, and a soloist or small ensemble suits the more intimate format. See our guide to non-religious funeral music and celebration of life music for fuller suggestions.

We cancel only in the most exceptional circumstances, and refund everything you have paid if we do.

How we build the ensemble

Singers who rehearse together tune to one another and breathe in the same places. Four singers meeting for the first time on the morning of the funeral are still working that out during the first hymn. We keep the team small, so the same voices sing together from one booking to the next, and Luca Wetherall auditions every singer before they work for a family.

Our singers on the day

Our singers are in the building 45 to 60 minutes before the service and check in with the funeral director on arrival. They wear plain dark dress unless you ask for something else, stand at the back rather than the front, and post nothing about the service.

Pricing

Funeral music questions

How much does a funeral choir cost in London?

A small choir of four costs £1,150, a quintet £1,400, a sextet £1,600 and a choir of eight £2,000. An organist adds £250. Each price covers up to two hours, including a rehearsal before the service, and travel within London. No VAT is added, and the written quote is the price you pay.

How much do singers charge for a funeral?

A soloist costs £275, and a small choir of four £1,150. Both lead the hymns and sing up to three pieces, and both prices include a rehearsal in the venue and travel within London. A soloist suits a short crematorium slot; four voices give the hymns full harmony.

Can you hire a choir for a funeral at our church?

Yes. A family can book us directly, or the funeral director can book us for you. Before the day we speak to the minister or celebrant and the funeral director, agree where the singers stand, and confirm the order of service. If the church has its own organist, we work with them.

How much notice do you need for a funeral?

We take bookings at short notice. Most funerals come to us with one or two weeks' notice, and we have covered services booked within forty-eight hours. Tell us the date and we confirm availability within one working day, usually the same day. If the service is in the next 48 hours, message us on WhatsApp, which is quickest.

Can you sing at a crematorium as well as a church?

Yes. We sing at crematoria, churches, cemetery chapels, and woodland burial sites across the UK. A soloist or small group suits the shorter crematorium slot; a larger choir suits a church service with hymns. We match the ensemble to the venue and the time you have.

Can you learn a song the family has asked for?

Yes. If a piece mattered to the person who died, we prepare it, whether it is a hymn, a pop song, or something from the classical repertoire. Send us the song and we arrange it for the voices you have booked.

Will the singers lead the hymns so guests feel confident?

That is one of the main reasons families book us. Trained voices carry the melody, so a congregation that half-remembers a hymn will still join in. It is the difference between a silent room and one that sings.

How does payment work for a funeral?

Half the fee is a non-refundable deposit that secures your date. You can pay the fee in one payment or in two equal instalments, with the second due 48 hours before the start; the first payment is due within seven days of confirmation. If the event is ten days away or fewer, the fee is a single payment. If you cancel more than thirty days before the date, we refund anything you have paid beyond the deposit. If you cancel thirty days or fewer before, the full fee is due. The full conditions are in our terms of booking.

Who will I be booking with?

The London Choral Service is the trading name of Alma Consort Ltd, and that is the company name you will see on your booking confirmation and invoice.
