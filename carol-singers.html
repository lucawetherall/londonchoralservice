Hire carol singers
Professional singers for parties, receptions, and December events

By Luca Wetherall, Artistic Director & Tutor in Music, University of Oxford

Last updated: August 2026

Singers who arrive, sing carols where your guests are already standing, and leave you to run the rest of the evening. Bookings start with four voices and run up to twelve, sung unaccompanied, in offices, hotels, apartment lobbies, at switch-ons and markets, and at parties at home. London and across the UK.

This page covers hiring singers for a party, a reception, or a lobby. If you are planning a full service with readings and congregational carols, see Christmas carol services.

Musicians from the Royal Academy, Royal College, and Britain’s leading conservatoires.

Where carol singers work

Most December bookings are office receptions and staff parties, where four singers work the room as people arrive and the drinks go round. Hotels book us for lobbies and festive afternoon teas. Towns and shopping streets book us for light switch-ons and Christmas markets. Managing agents book us for residents’ evenings and community events in apartment buildings, and families book us for parties at home.

The singers can roam through the crowd, stopping wherever a group has gathered, or stay in one position for sets of three or four carols. Roaming suits a drinks reception where guests arrive across an hour. Stationary sets suit a seated dinner, or a switch-on with a countdown to sing up to.

Everything is sung a cappella, so there is no stage to build, no power to find, and no PA to hire. Give the group a corner of a lobby or the foot of a staircase and they will fill the space. Outdoors is fine as well: markets, courtyards, and switch-ons are ordinary December work, though we will ask for cover from rain and somewhere warm to wait between sets.

Dress is your call. Concert dress for a formal evening, all black for a lobby set that should not draw the eye, ordinary clothes for a market, or Christmas jumpers if you want the singers to look like part of the party. We have Santa hats if you want them. You may know them as carollers (or carolers, in the American spelling); either way it is the same trained group.

The repertoire runs from the traditional carols everyone knows — Ding Dong Merrily on High, Silent Night, Hark! The Herald Angels Sing — through the quieter Advent pieces to a cappella arrangements of the popular Christmas songs people expect at a party. Tell us the tone you are after and Luca sets the running order against it.

We are based in London, and that is where most of the work sits: office lobbies in the City, hotels in Mayfair, receptions in Canary Wharf, residents’ evenings in the riverside buildings. We travel for the rest of the country too. Travel within Greater London is included in the price, and anything further is quoted with the booking.

Where the venue has a piano, you can book the choir with background piano between the sets: the pianist keeps the room from falling silent while the singers rest, then drops out as they come back in. If there is no piano, we can bring a keyboard and speaker for an extra charge, quoted upfront in your written quote. Neither is necessary. The singing stands on its own.

Frequently asked questions

How long do carol singers perform for?

A standard Christmas booking runs up to an hour and a half including breaks. That is usually three sets of twenty minutes or so, or continuous singing across a shorter reception. Longer bookings carry a premium, quoted upfront. Where the venue has a piano, a pianist can cover the gaps between the sets.

Do carol singers need microphones or amplification?

No. Trained voices carry a hotel lobby or a full office floor unamplified, so there is no stage to build, no PA to hire, and nothing to plug in. Put the singers where the ceiling is high and the floor is hard, such as a staircase, an atrium, or a gallery, and the sound reaches the whole room.

How many carol singers should we book for our venue?

Size the group to the space. Four singers suit a lobby, an office reception, or a room of up to 150. Six fill a mid-sized hall or a foyer with a steady hum; eight for a busy bar or a crowd talking across you. Eight cover a large hall or a church of 400, and twelve are for several hundred guests or a ticketed concert.

Can guests sing along or request a carol?

Yes to both. Song sheets on the well-known carols turn the last ten minutes into a singalong, which works particularly well at residents’ evenings and family parties. Requests are welcome where they sit within the repertoire. Send them a week or two ahead and Luca will place them in the sets.

Can carol singers perform outdoors in the cold?

Yes. Markets, courtyards, and light switch-ons are ordinary December work. We ask for cover from rain, since sheet music and wind do not mix, and somewhere warm to wait between sets. In hard frost we sing shorter sets more often rather than standing out for a solid hour.

What do carol singers wear?

Whatever suits your event. Concert dress for a formal evening, all black for a lobby set that should not draw the eye, ordinary clothes for a market or a family party, or Christmas jumpers if you want the singers to look festive. Santa hats are available if you ask for them.
