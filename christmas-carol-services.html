Once in Royal David’s City, sung by our full choir

By Luca Wetherall, Artistic Director & Tutor in Music, University of Oxford

Companies, charities, and livery companies often book a church for an evening in December and then need musicians to fill it. You settle the date with the incumbent; we bring the choir and, if you want the carols accompanied, an organist.

The singers trained at the Royal Academy of Music, the Royal College of Music, and Britain’s other conservatoires.

What we do at a carol service

The first thing we agree is which carols the congregation sings and which the choir takes alone. Four singers will lead a room of 150 through Hark! The Herald Angels Sing; eight will carry a church of 400. In between, the choir sings the pieces a congregation cannot: the Willcocks descants, Poston’s Jesus Christ the Apple Tree, an unaccompanied Gaudete. Six of each is the usual balance for an hour.

The running order is yours to choose. A liturgical service follows the Nine Lessons and Carols pattern from King’s College: a bidding prayer, nine readings, a carol after each, and a blessing at the end. A secular running order drops the prayers and puts a chief executive, a patron, or a beneficiary in the reading slots, usually with the appeal before the last carol. Either runs to about an hour. Our Christmas carols guide covers the carols most services draw on.

A full carol service usually calls for a choir of eight with an organist. We can bring an organist who knows the instrument, added to any choir for £250, to accompany the congregational carols and play the voluntaries before and after. The choir can also sing unaccompanied throughout.

We deal with the building direct. Once you confirm the booking, we contact the clergy, the verger, and the church’s organist about access, rehearsal time, and the order of service, then send you the arrival and rehearsal times. The choir rehearses in the church on the day, before the doors open.

O Little Town of Bethlehem — Full Choir

Phillips Brooks’s words of 1868 to Forest Green, the tune Vaughan Williams took from a Surrey folk song, sung by eight voices. Congregations know it, and we sing it mid-service, between two louder carols.

O Little Town of Bethlehem (words Phillips Brooks), sung by our full choir.

Ding Dong! Merrily on High — Full Choir

A French dance tune from 1589 with words George Ratcliffe Woodward added in 1924, sung by eight voices. It sits well after a reading, with the congregation joining the Gloria refrain.

Frequently asked questions

Can you help us put together the order of service?

Yes. Luca drafts the running order with you and marks which carols the congregation sings and which the choir sings alone. He can follow the Nine Lessons and Carols pattern or a secular order with readers of your choosing, and we send you the final music list for the printed order of service.

Do we need the church’s permission for a carol service with a visiting choir?

Yes. You arrange the church and the date with the incumbent, and we sing there with the church’s permission. Once you confirm the booking, we contact the clergy and the verger about access, rehearsal time, and the order of service, so you do not have to arrange it yourself.

Can you bring an organist for the carol service?

Yes. We can bring an organist who knows the instrument, added to any choir for £250, to accompany the congregational carols and play voluntaries before and after the service. We coordinate with the church’s organist and verger.

When does the choir rehearse for a carol service?

On the day, in the church, before the doors open. The singers arrive knowing the music, so we use the rehearsal to settle the acoustic, the positions, and the hand-overs between choir, organist, and congregation.

Will the choir lead the congregation in the carols?

Yes. Four singers will lead a room of 150 through Hark! The Herald Angels Sing, and eight will carry a church of 400. We agree beforehand which carols everyone sings and which the choir takes alone; six of each is the usual balance for an hour.

How do we pay for a carol service choir?

Half the fee is a non-refundable deposit that secures your date. You can pay the fee in one payment or in two equal instalments, with the second due 48 hours before the start; the first payment is due within seven days of confirmation. If the event is ten days away or fewer, the fee is a single payment. If you cancel more than thirty days before the date, we refund anything you have paid beyond the deposit. If you cancel thirty days or fewer before, the full fee is due. The full conditions are in our terms of booking.

How much does a choir for a carol service cost?

A Small Choir of four is £1,150, a full choir of eight £2,000, and a chorus of twelve £3,000, and an organist adds £250. A booking covers the length of the service, and our prices include travel within Greater London. Christmas Eve and Christmas Day carry a premium of around 25%.
